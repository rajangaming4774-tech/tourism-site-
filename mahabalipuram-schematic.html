What this drawing commits to

A schematic is where an invented dimension would slip through unnoticed and then get built, so this one draws only what the sources give. A monument with a published height and a published storey count is drawn as massing. Everything else is a position on the ground with no height at all — and the reason is written down rather than smoothed over.

Heights are cited totals. How the height splits between storeys, and how far each one steps in, is stylised — a constant-slope tower stepped at the storey lines. The storey count is cited; the profile is not, and that is one of the things this drawing is asking you to approve.

Elevations and sections — 0 sheets

Plan, elevation and section for every monument that has cited dimensions, all drawn to one common scale so they can be compared with each other — an 11 m tower and a 5.5 m hut should not occupy the same space on the page. Every line is projected from the same per-tier geometry the 3D massing extrudes; nothing is redrawn by hand.

Do not scale the plan. The outline is the OpenStreetMap way and every one runs wider than the published plan, because OSM maps the visible footing and apron rather than the building line. Both figures are printed on each sheet. The sections are solid because no interior dimension is published for any of these — the rathas are monoliths and largely are solid, but each has a small shrine cell that is not drawn.

Every monument OpenStreetMap records inside the sheet is marked with a ground cross, so the layout can be judged complete. A cross claims a location and nothing else.

What happens after approval

  1. Layout and proportion — the footprints and cited heights above. If these are right, the site plan is right.
  2. The twelve scenes — play them through. Each is a camera pose on one continuous path; approving them fixes what the finished view will show.
  3. Then modelling — and only for monuments in the first table, unless a source is found for one in the second.

Outlines and positions: OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0. Elevation: Mapzen / AWS Terrain Tiles, 2 m contours from a ~30 m DEM — good for landform, not for a monument's own base. Heights as cited per row.
